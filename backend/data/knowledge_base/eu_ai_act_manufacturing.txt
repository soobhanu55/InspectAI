The European Union Artificial Intelligence Act (EU AI Act) establishes a legal framework for AI systems within the EU.
For manufacturing operations, AI systems are primarily categorized under "Minimal Risk" (Article 6).
Quality control and defect detection systems that do not interact with consumers, manipulate human behavior, or act as safety components in critical infrastructure fall under this category.
These systems can be deployed without mandatory conformity assessments, though voluntary codes of conduct are encouraged.

However, if an AI system in manufacturing is used as a safety component for machinery (regulated under the Machinery Directive 2006/42/EC), it may be classified as "High-Risk" under Annex III.
High-Risk AI systems require strict compliance with Risk Management (Article 9), Data Governance (Article 10), Technical Documentation (Article 11), Transparency (Article 13), Human Oversight (Article 14), and a robust Quality Management System (Article 17).
Compliance deadlines for high-risk systems are set for August 2026.
Manufacturers must ensure that AI systems detecting defects in parts that could compromise the final product's structural safety (e.g., automotive braking components) undergo rigorous conformity assessments before market deployment.
